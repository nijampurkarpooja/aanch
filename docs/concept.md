# Aanch: product concept

Checkpoint 1. **Status: approved 26 Nov 2026.**
Imagery and prompts: [art-direction.md](art-direction.md).

## 1. In one line

**Aanch** (आँच, the steady heat of a flame) is a Bengaluru studio that makes stoneware for the Indian
table, block-printed and glazed by hand, sold online across India.

## 2. Business concept

One studio selling its own work direct to customers. It is not a marketplace. The kiln is fired about every ten days, and
each firing is a numbered batch. Firings are how stock arrives, how restocks are announced and how a
piece is traced.

| Assumption | Decision |
|---|---|
| Base and ship-from | Bengaluru, Karnataka: CGST + SGST inside Karnataka, IGST everywhere else |
| Market | Pan-India wherever a carrier serves the PIN; marketing aimed at the six metros and tier-1 cities |
| Pricing | INR, GST-inclusive (MRP convention), stored as integer paise |
| GST | Ceramic tableware, HSN 6912: 5% since 22 Sep 2025 (to confirm against CBIC at Checkpoint 3) |
| Shipping | Free from ₹2,500, otherwise ₹149 |
| COD | Up to ₹5,000, ₹99 fee, only on serviceable PINs |
| Returns | Free replacement or refund for transit breakage reported with a photo within 48 h; 7-day returns on unused pieces, return shipping deducted; Seconds are final sale |
| Average order target | About ₹3,000 |
| Demo status | A portfolio demo of a fictional brand: no real orders, payments or deliveries. Payments run in the gateway's test mode only (section 14). |
| Budget | ₹0 for development, tools and hosting: free tiers and test modes only, no custom domain |

## 3. Audience

- **The first real home.** Urban, 26–40, cooks Indian food at home. Replacing melamine and
  mismatched steel with things they like using daily. Finds brands on Instagram, pays by UPI, cares
  that glazes are lead-free.
- **The gift buyer.** Weddings, housewarmings (griha pravesh), Diwali: 4–8 gifts a year. Needs
  wrap, a message, a price-free gift note and a dependable delivery date.
- **The studio team (admin).** Founder (reports, pricing), operations lead (firings, stock,
  packing), support (orders, claims, refunds).

## 4. Positioning

Between mass retail (₹300 mugs) and luxury lifestyle (₹3,000+ mugs).

> For people who cook Indian food at home, Aanch makes stoneware shaped around how we eat and printed
> with the patterns we grew up with. The gravy stays on the thali, and the chai stays hot in the kulhad.

## 5. Personality, story, voice

**Story (fictional).** A ceramicist and a former line cook started Aanch in 2021 in a garage in north
Bengaluru. The cook couldn't find handmade plates that held a dal. Today eight people throw, print,
glaze and pack. The wooden print blocks are carved by a family of block-makers in Pethapur, Gujarat.

**Personality:** warm, exact, a little funny. It sounds like a potter who also cooks.

- Do: give capacities in food ("holds one generous serving of dal"), name the firing, say what's
  sold out and when it's back.
- Don't: *artisanal, curated, elevate, timeless, handcrafted with love.* No exclamation marks in
  the UI. Errors say what happened and what to do next.

## 6. Indian craft in the product, not just on the site

Every piece has a **Finish**: either a plain glaze or a **Chhaap** (छाप, a print). The site's
patterns are the products' patterns, so the brand and the catalogue are one system.

**Glazes (5)**

| Glaze | Look |
|---|---|
| Neel | Glossy cobalt that pools dark in hollows and breaks pale on rims (the signature) |
| Haldi | Satin turmeric ochre with iron speckle |
| Monsoon | Translucent grey-green celadon with a fine crackle |
| Laterite | Satin iron red-brown, dark speckle, like laterite stone |
| Chuna | Matte limewash white, chalky and dry to the touch |

**Chhaap prints (4)**: each is made with a real ceramic technique that has a textile cousin.

| Print | Motif | Technique | Textile cousin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buti | Small five-petal flowers, scattered | Cobalt slip stamped with a carved wooden block, on Chuna | Bagru/Sanganeri block print |
| Jaal | Interlocking ogee lattice | Wax resist: Chuna lines through Neel | Dabu mud-resist |
| Bel | Trailing vine, rims only | Block-stamped cobalt on Haldi | Border (kinar) prints |
| Leheriya | Diagonal ripples | Sgraffito: lines scratched through Laterite to the pale clay | Leheriya tie-dye |

**Cultural care rules.** Use generic motif vocabulary (buti, bel, jaal, keri, leheriya) only. No sacred
symbols or deities on anything you eat from. No community-specific art forms (Warli, Gond, Madhubani)
unless it's a named, credited collaboration. Craft names describe inspiration; they never claim a
GI-tagged origin.

## 8. Site vocabulary

The rule: **brand words for places and things; plain words for actions.** Buttons and checkout stay
exactly as people expect.

| Aanch word | Means | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Firing, Firing No. 112 | A stock batch | Product page, admin stock receipts |
| New firing | New arrivals | Main nav |
| Kiln calendar | Restock schedule | Main nav, sold-out states |
| Finish | The variant attribute (glaze or print) | Product page, filters |
| Chhaap | Print collection and story | Nav, collection, product page |
| Kiln Seconds | Flawed pieces at a discount | Nav, labels |
| Your shelf | Wishlist ("Save to shelf") | Header, account |
| Studio | About, process, journal ("Studio notes") | Nav, footer |
| Holds | Capacity, said in food | Product page |

Kept plain: Cart, Add to cart, Checkout, Pay, Account, Orders, Track order, Report a breakage, every
order status. Empty and error states carry the voice: an empty cart reads "Nothing on the table yet"
with a link to shop, and the 404 reads "This page cracked in the kiln" with a way back.

## 9. The experience: a familiar shop with things worth touching

**The shell stays familiar:** a standard header (logo, Shop menu, search, shelf, cart), a filterable
grid, a product page with the gallery on the left and the buy box on the right, a cart drawer, a
standard checkout, and order tracking. Every interaction below sits on top of that shell, answers a
person's action, and has a plain fallback (keyboard, screen reader, reduced motion, slow network).

| # | Interaction | What happens | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The table from above** (home hero) | An overhead cinemagraph of a laid meal (steam from the dal). Hotspots on each piece open a quick view with Add to cart. Chai / Thali / Pantry / Festive swap the table, crossfading. | Shop-by-meal is the positioning; the hero *is* the store's best collection page |
| 2 | **Kiln calendar** | The current firing's stage (Throwing, Drying, Bisque, Glazing, Firing, Cooling, On the shelf) plotted on a drawn kiln temperature curve up to 1,240°C, with what's in it and the date it lands. Sold-out variants link here with "Notify me next firing". | Turns "sold out" from a dead end into anticipation. Driven by real admin data. |
| 3 | **Will it hold?** | Every product has a potter's profile drawing (half cross-section), drawn in code from data. A Fill control (Dal, Rice, Chai, Dahi) fills the section to its real ml, with dimensions marked. | Answers the real question for Indian food: depth, not just diameter |
| 4 | **Finish that changes the picture** | Swatches are real glaze and print close-ups, not colour dots. Picking one swaps the whole gallery. On the close-up, moving the pointer moves the light across the glaze (two light directions, crossfaded). | Glaze is what people buy; let them see it break on the rim |
| 5 | **Shelf view** | A toggle on listings: pieces stand on a shelf line at **true relative scale** (the katori beside the thali), sized from their dimensions. The grid stays the default. | Scale is the most common doubt buying tableware online |
| 6 | **The stamp** | Add to cart presses a block-print stamp onto the cart icon, with slight misregistration like a real print. The order confirmation is stamped with the firing's chop mark, and the gift note is previewed as a printed card. | One tactile signature, reused, so it feels made by hand |
| 7 | **Clay to table** (one scroll story) | Five full-width chapters (wedge, throw, stamp, glaze, open the kiln), each a short video loop with a still poster. This is the only scroll-driven motion on the site. | The story behind the price, told once |

**Motion rules.** Motion follows an action (a pick, a hover, a press, a toggle). The one scroll story
lives on the homepage; nothing else fades in on scroll. Ambient video runs only in the hero and the
chapters, with a pause control, poster first, and it rests under reduced motion or Data Saver.

## 10. Visual direction: "Printed in one ink"

**The idea.** The site is printed the way a Bagru textile is: **one cobalt ink on unbleached-cotton
sand**. Text, rules, icons, motifs and drawings are all Neel ink. Colour comes only from the pots and
the food in the photographs, so the product is always the most colourful thing on screen. This is the
one bold thing; everything around it is quiet.

**Palette** (tints checked for contrast)

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast on sand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cotton | `#E6DBC7` | Page ground | n/a |
| Cotton-deep | `#DDD0B9` | Surfaces, image wells | n/a |
| Neel ink | `#062B8A` | Text, rules, icons, primary buttons, motifs | 8.9:1 |
| Neel 75 | `#3E5799` | Secondary text | 5.1:1 |
| Madder | `#A8322A` | Second ink: Seconds and sale prices, errors (always with an icon and text) | 4.9:1 |
| Chuna | `#F5F1E8` | Admin ground, inputs, cards that need lift | n/a |
| Moss / Turmeric-dark | `#2F6B4F` / `#7A4F00` | Success / warning text | 4.6 / 5.2:1 |

**Type: set in Indian-made type**

- **Eczar** (Vaibhav Singh, Rosetta; Latin and Devanagari, OFL) for display and headings, 600–800. It's
  sturdy and calligraphic, with the weight of a carved block. Large headlines take a faint ink-press
  texture (an SVG filter) in the hero only.
- **Anek Latin** (Ek Type, Mumbai; OFL, width and weight axes) for UI and body, 400/500. Its
  condensed width (wdth 75) with tabular figures handles prices, SKUs, firing numbers and admin
  tables, instead of a monospace.
- Devanagari words (आँच, छाप) appear only as a graphic element: one huge stamped word per page at most.
- Sentence case everywhere. No all-caps labels, no eyebrows over headings, no arrows on links.

**Layout: built like a saree.** A *kinar* (border), a *field* and a *pallu* (the decorated end).

- **Kinar:** a thin stamped Bel border under the header. It's the only ornament on most screens.
- **Field:** a 12-column grid, text left-aligned, generous space. Listings are calm, and products
  sit on a faint shelf line.
- **Pallu:** the footer, the densest printed area on the site (full-bleed Jaal), with the newsletter
  ("Get the kiln calendar by email") and links in plain cotton panels.

**Imagery.** All AI-generated, under one shared style block, and disclosed in the footer's demo line

## 12. Admin operational requirements

- **Roles:** Owner (everything), Operations (catalogue, firings, stock, fulfilment), Support
  (orders, claims, customers, refunds up to a set limit).
- **Today:** orders to pack, COD orders to confirm, open breakage claims, low stock, firing status.
- **Catalogue:** products, variants (finish × size, price per variant), finishes as managed
  attributes, collections, profile data with a live drawing preview.
- **Product lifecycle:** every product and every variant is **Draft**, **Active** or **Archived**.
  - Draft: being prepared, not visible on the storefront.
  - Active: on sale.
  - Archived: off the storefront and out of search, and its link shows "No longer made" with
    similar pieces. It can be restored at any time.
  - Delete: only for something that was never ordered (made by mistake). Anything with orders can
    only be archived.
  - Order lines keep a copy of the name, finish, price and GST rate at purchase, so later edits,
    archiving or price changes never rewrite past orders, invoices or reports.
  - Owner and Operations can do all of this, and each change goes in the audit log.
- **Firings and stock:** plan a firing with its stage dates (feeds the kiln calendar), receive a
  firing to add stock in bulk, adjust with a reason, a full stock ledger, low-stock thresholds.
- **Orders:** status pipeline, packing slips, carrier booking with tracking numbers, COD
  reconciliation, cancellations, full or partial refunds, and a breakage-claim queue with photo review.
- **Customers:** profile, orders, internal notes, data export and deletion requests (DPDP Act).
- **Discounts:** codes (percentage or fixed), minimum order, usage limits, expiry, no stacking.
- **Content:** homepage tables and hotspots, and a firing note on the kiln calendar. Journal posts
  live in the repo. There's no separate announcement bar: the demo strip holds that slot (section 14).
- **Reports:** sales by day, product and finish; AOV; COD vs prepaid; RTO rate; refunds; GST summary
  by HSN and state for GSTR-1.
- **Settings:** shipping rates, COD limits, GSTIN, invoice series, business address.
- **Audit log:** every change to price, stock, refunds, roles and settings.

## 14. Demo notice

Aanch is a demo, and the site says so in three places only: above the header, in the footer and
at payment. Each uses the studio's voice and asks people not to enter their own details.

| Where | Copy | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Strip above the header | "Aanch is a demo studio. Nothing here is fired, shipped or charged, so use made-up details, not your own." Link: About this demo. | Not sticky: it scrolls away with the top of the page. It can be hidden, and stays hidden for the visit. |
| Footer, one line | "A demo studio built for a portfolio: no real orders, payments or deliveries. Product photographs and films are AI-generated." | It also carries the imagery disclosure, so there's no second credit line. |
| Payment step | "Test kiln: no money moves. Pay with the test card or UPI ID shown here, never your own." The gateway's test details are shown beside it. | The only notice in checkout. |

**No repeats on the same screen**

- Checkout uses a focused layout with no strip and no full footer, so the payment notice is the
  only one there.
- Pages short enough for the strip and the footer to share a screen (sign-in, empty cart, 404)
  drop the footer's demo sentence and keep only the imagery sentence.
- Nowhere else: not in the cart drawer, modals, product pages or emails.

**Keeping personal data out**

- The checkout address step and sign-up offer **Use sample details**, which fills a made-up
  name, address and PIN, so testing never needs real data.
- The **About this demo** page explains the project and that demo data is cleared regularly. How
  it's cleared is a Checkpoint 3 decision.
